Jewelry History

The Mixtec Masters (900 – 1521 AD)

There is significant evidence that the Mixtec people of ancient Mexico were the most skilled goldsmiths in the Americas. Centered in the Oaxaca Valley, they mastered the "lost-wax" casting technique to create gold jewelry that mirrored the intricate carvings of stone stelas (monuments) found in their ceremonial centers. --Source

The Heart of the Craft: Guadalajara

While the Mixtecs established the foundation, Guadalajara became the heart of Mexico's jewelry industry during the colonial era and remains so in 2026. This city transformed ancient techniques into a global powerhouse of design.

  • Jewelry Capital: Guadalajara currently produces 70% of Mexico's fine jewelry.
  • Technique Fusion: The city is the birthplace of "Mestizo" jewelry, blending Spanish filigree with indigenous gold casting.
  • Global Export: It serves as the main gateway for Mexican gold and silver to reach international markets, including our headquarters in Los Angeles.

The Gold of Monte Albán (1932)

The 1932 discovery of Tomb 7 at Monte Albán remains one of the most important archeological finds in history. It revealed hundreds of pieces of gold jewelry, proving that Mexico has a deep, ancestral connection to precious metals and high-end craftsmanship. This history of "Stela" carving and gold-work is the foundation of our brand.

From Stone to Carbon

The connection between ancient stelas and modern jewelry lies in their geometry. Just as the ancients carved limestone into precise pillars, modern jewelry relies on the geometric precision of the diamond. Today, we have moved from carving stone in the year 1000 AD to cultivating carbon and refined gold in 2026.

Sustainable Innovation

With the advances in modern technology, the jewelry industry has changed significantly. Stela Marea utilizes Lab-Grown Diamonds to produce stones that are chemically identical to mined diamonds but with a smaller environmental footprint. --Source
